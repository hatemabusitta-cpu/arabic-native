## 3. Dialects today

### 3.1 The main families (quick markers)

| Region | "What?" | "I want" | "Now" | "Very" | Other markers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Najdi (Riyadh, Qassim) | وش | أبي / أبغى | الحين | مرّة، حيل | ق as /g/; ك → /ts/ in feminine for some speakers (عليتس); زين (good), ترى (by the way / you know) |
| Hijazi (Jeddah, Makkah, Madinah) | إيش | أبغى | دحين | مرّة | كمان (also), يا شيخ (really?), عاد; ج as /j/ |
| Gulf (Kuwait, UAE, Bahrain, Qatar) | شنو / شو | أبي / أبا | الحين | وايد | ج → /y/ in some (ريّال for رجّال "man"), عيش = rice |
| Levantine (Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Palestine) | شو | بدّي | هلّق / هلأ | كتير | منيح (good), ق → glottal stop in cities |
| Egyptian | إيه | عايز | دلوقتي | أوي | ج → /g/, ق → glottal stop, كويس (good), عيش = bread |
| Maghrebi (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia) | شنو / واش | بغيت | دابا / توّا | بزاف | heavy French loans, ماشي = "not" in Morocco |
| Sudanese | شنو | داير | هسّي / هسع | شديد | زول (person), سمح (nice/beautiful), ياخ (hey/dude), كيفنك (how are you) |
| Iraqi | شنو / شكو | أريد | هسّه | هواية | ماكو (there isn't), چ for ك |

[common knowledge; markers are typical rather than universal]

Saudi brand and platform names show the grammar in action: **أبشر** (Absher, "rest assured / at your service", imperative used as a promise), **توكلنا** (Tawakkalna, "we put our trust [in God]"), **صحّتي** (Sehhaty, "my health", the possessive ياء), **نفاذ** (Nafath, "access"), **جاهز** (Jahez, "ready"). These are short, root-transparent, and emotionally loaded, which is a good model for naming.

### 3.3 "White dialect" (اللهجة البيضاء)

- **Definition:** a Saudi (or pan-Gulf) dialect stripped of strongly regional markers, so that people from Najd, Hijaz, the South, the East and the North all hear it as "ours" and nobody hears it as someone else's dialect. [verified: Al-Madina, Majalla]
- **Evidence:** a Saudi researcher in consumer behavior reported that about **85%** of Saudi respondents preferred the non-regional white dialect in X/Twitter ads over MSA or a specific regional dialect, and listed being understood across all regions as a condition for a successful ad. The original study could not be accessed to check sample and method. [verified as reported: Al-Madina]
- **Ongoing debate:** Al-Majalla describes Saudi ad content swinging between Fusha, white dialect and full dialect. One camp wants comprehensible Fusha everywhere. Another accepts dialect for acted or video ads aimed at locals while keeping written copy in Fusha. Saudi dialect has grown much more visible in digital content since the 2010s. Al-Watan reports that some dialect ads became objects of online mockery and were pulled. [verified: Majalla, Al-Watan]
- **What white dialect sounds like** (illustrative):
  - Uses widely shared words: أبغى/أبي، الحين، زين، مرّة، حيّاك، ترى، يعني، كذا، وش رأيك.
  - Avoids strongly local features: Najdi /ts/ (عليتس), Hijazi دحين, southern or northern vocabulary, heavy Bedouin terms.
  - Often keeps MSA grammar words (الذي → اللي is fine, but full sentences stay simple).
  - Example: «اطلب الحين، ويوصلك طلبك بسرعة» ("Order now and your order reaches you fast").
- **When Gulf brands use what** [observed / verify]:

| Context | Typical register |
|---|---|
| Government services, legal text, banking terms, T&Cs, contracts | MSA, formal |
| App UI labels and buttons (including consumer apps) | Mostly short MSA (حفظ، إلغاء، تسجيل الدخول) |
| Push notifications, social posts, ads, influencer scripts for consumer brands | White Saudi dialect, often playful |
| National campaigns, tourism, entertainment | Mix: MSA headlines plus dialect voice-over, or white dialect throughout |
| B2B, LinkedIn, press releases | MSA |
| Ramadan and Eid campaigns | Warm MSA or dialect, with religious greetings |
| Pan-Arab brand serving several countries | MSA, or separate dialect versions per market |

- **Rule for the skill:** default to **simple MSA** for UI and anything permanent. Use **white Saudi dialect** for social, notifications and ads when the audience is Saudi. Never mix dialects in one piece (Egyptian «عايز» next to Saudi «أبغى»).

### 3.4 Sudanese Arabic (the author's roots)

- Sudanese Arabic shares much with Egyptian and Hijazi Arabic but has distinct vocabulary: زول (person), داير (want), هسّي (now), سمح (good, beautiful), شديد (very/strong), ياخ (dude), قروش (money), بكرة (tomorrow), حبوبة (grandmother). [common knowledge]
- **Watch-outs:** داير in MSA means "rotating/circling" (دائر), and سمح in MSA means "allowed". A Saudi or Levantine reader may misread Sudanese copy, so keep public copy in MSA or Saudi white dialect unless it targets Sudanese audiences.

## 6. Modern and new-generation Arabic

### 6.1 Internet Arabic and Arabizi (عربيزي / Franco)

- **Arabizi** writes dialect in Latin letters with digits for Arabic sounds. It arose with SMS, chat and email when devices lacked Arabic support. The name blends عربي and إنجليزي. [verified: Wikipedia mirror, Arabic for Nerds]

| Digit | Letter | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | ء (hamza; in Egypt also ق) | so2al = سؤال |
| 3 | ع | 3arabi = عربي |
| 3' | غ | 3'ali = غالي |
| 5 (or 7') | خ | 5alas = خلاص |
| 6 | ط | 6ayeb = طيب (Gulf) |
| 7 | ح | 7abibi = حبيبي |
| 8 | ق (rare) | |
| 9 | ص | 9ba7 = صباح (Gulf) |

- Conventions differ by dialect: Gulf Arabizi uses 6 and 9 more, and Egyptian Arabizi (Franco) uses 2 heavily. [verified]
- **Status in 2026:** Arabic keyboards are universal, so Arabizi has declined in public posts. It survives in DMs, usernames, gaming, and among some Levantine and Egyptian youth. Brands occasionally use it for a playful or nostalgic effect. [observed]
- **Rule for the skill:** never use Arabizi in UI or formal copy. Use it only if the user explicitly wants a youth or retro tone, and match the target dialect's digit conventions.

### 6.2 Code-switching with English

- Saudi and Gulf youth, especially in tech, marketing and private schools, mix English freely: «خلني أcheck الإيميل», «الميتنق بكرة», «الديدلاين قريب». English terms are often written in Arabic script with Arabic morphology: يسيّف (to save), فلّو (follow him), منشن، ستوري، لايف، بلوك (to block), مود (mood), فايب (vibe), ترند / تريند. [observed]
- **When it works:** influencer scripts, startup social posts, recruitment ads for tech roles.
- **When it fails:** government, banking, older audiences, anything permanent. It also ages fast.
- **Spelling of borrowed terms in Arabic script varies** («ترند» / «تريند»; «إيميل» / «ايميل» / «البريد الإلكتروني»). Pick one per brand and keep it in the glossary.

### 6.3 Saudi/Gulf youth slang (handle with care; it ages fast)

[observed / verify with a current Saudi teen or young adult before using]

| Word | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **كفو** | "well done, you nailed it" | Widely used, fairly stable; brands use it |
| **رهيب** / **يهبّل** / **خرافي** | awesome | رهيب means "terrifying" in MSA |
| **فلّة** | fun, chill | Possibly dated |
| **طقطقة / يطقطق** | teasing, roasting, banter | Very common in Saudi social media |
| **هياط / مهايط** | showing off, bragging | Negative |
| **سالفة** / «وش السالفة؟» | story, matter / "what's going on?" | Stable |
| **ترى** | "just so you know, by the way" | Stable discourse marker |
| **عز الله** | Najdi: "truly / oh no" | Regional |
| **ما عليك زود** | "you're the best, no one tops you" | Stable compliment |
| **يجي** | "it works, deal" (agreement) | Reported by Okaz [verified] |
| **الزبدة** | "the bottom line" | Reported by Okaz [verified] |
| **إكسبلور** / «طلعت إكسبلور» | went viral on the Explore page | Platform slang |
| **كرنج** | cringe | English loan, trendy |
| **خخخخ** | Gulf laughter (alongside ههههه) | Typing convention |

- A 2023 Princess Nourah University project surveyed Saudi teens on Gen Z TikTok terms and found that the terms carry **new pragmatic meanings** that older speakers miss. [verified as reported: Scribd abstract]
- Okaz describes youth speech as hard for older people to follow at first hearing, while sociologists see it as natural language change. [verified]
- **Rule:** at most one or two slang items per piece, and only where the audience is clearly young. When in doubt, use warm white dialect instead of slang.

### 6.4 Emoji, laughter and tone

- Laughter: «ههههه» is pan-Arab; «خخخخ» is Gulf; 😂 is fading among youth in favour of 💀 and 😭 (a global trend). [observed]
- 🌚 (dark moon face) is popular in Arabic social media for mischief or a knowing look. [observed]
- 🙂 can read as passive-aggressive.
- Greetings carry tone: «هلا والله» (very warm, Gulf), «يا هلا» (warm), «مرحبًا» (neutral), «السلام عليكم» (respectful, safe for all ages).
- Religious seasons set the tone: «رمضان كريم» / «رمضان مبارك», «مبارك عليكم الشهر» (Gulf), «عيدكم مبارك», «كل عام وأنتم بخير». Avoid humor that mocks fasting or prayer.

### 6.5 Platform registers [observed]

| Platform | Typical Arabic |
|---|---|
| **Snapchat** (very strong in Saudi Arabia) | Spoken dialect, very casual, short captions, voice-led |
| **TikTok** | Spoken dialect; captions in dialect; trending audio and catchphrases; Arabizi rare |
| **X** | Mix: MSA for news and opinion, dialect for humor; threads called «ثريد» |
| **Instagram** | Brand captions in light MSA or white dialect; hashtags in Arabic with underscores (#موسم_الرياض) |
| **LinkedIn** | MSA or English |
| **WhatsApp business messages** | Polite white dialect or simple MSA; short |

### 6.6 Current trends, 2024–2026 [observed / verify]

- **Dialect goes mainstream in national branding.** Saudi entertainment, tourism and events use Saudi dialect openly, especially in video.
- **State investment in Arabic:** the Ministry of Culture declared year-themes including the **Year of Arabic Calligraphy** (2020–2021) and the **Year of Arabic Poetry** (2023). The King Salman Global Academy runs terminology and computational-linguistics programs. [partly verified: KSAA]
- **Arabic AI:** Saudi Arabia (SDAIA's ALLaM, and HUMAIN's chat products) and the UAE (Jais) launched Arabic-first language models. The result is a flood of AI-written Arabic with telltale signs: calques, «يتم», clichés from 5.5, «في عالم اليوم المتسارع», «لا شك أن», and overuse of «رحلة».
- **Western digits and Latin terms keep growing in tech UI**, while Eastern digits persist in print and formal documents (see 7.6).
